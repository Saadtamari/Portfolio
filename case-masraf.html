01 / The problem

Finance apps don’t
speak to Arab freelancers.

Freelancers across the Arab world juggle invoices, clients, expenses and Zakat in tools built for someone else, in English, with terms that don’t fit Islamic finance.

We wanted an app you could simply talk to, in Arabic, that did the work on screen while it answered.

03 / Decisions worth explaining

Why it’s
built this way.

01

Voice and UI, together

Every voice intent maps to a page and a UI action, so the screen changes as the app speaks. You can also use it like a normal app.

02

Islamic finance as a foundation

A Zakat page, a purification ledger and the right words everywhere: “ربح” for profit, never “interest”.

03

Spec before code

A full requirements spec first: 12 tables with SQL, 4 storage buckets with access policies, 19 endpoints and 16 voice intents.

04

Fast enough to feel live

Speech-to-text on Groq and a fast hosted model for reasoning keep the loop short enough to feel like a conversation.

04 / What we shipped

A prototype that
talks back.

  • Eight pages with Arabic names: documents, dashboard, invoices, clients, expenses, contracts, Zakat and reports.
  • Mobile and desktop interfaces, reviewed and fixed for currency consistency and voice-driven navigation.
  • Built by a team of four for SalamHack 2026.
  • A direction I still believe in: Arabic-first means the logic and the language, not a translated label.
